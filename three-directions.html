Your brief has more than one world in it

Glass, animal print, botanicals, old paper, saturated colour — I collapsed them all into one ceramic idea twice and it wasn't right. So here they are pulled apart instead. Each of these is a different spine with the others as accents. None is a variation on the last two.

I

Cathedral Electric

Stained glass as the spine. Black leading, jewel panes lit from behind, colour that glows rather than sits. This is the "polished electric" half of what you said, taken seriously — saturation at full strength on near-black, with gold light bleeding off the type. Botanicals appear as leaded flower panels; texture comes from glass, not print.

II

Leopard & Silk

Animal print as the spine, treated like a fashion house rather than a novelty. Leopard in gold on oxblood, silk sheen across every cover, engraved Roman caps, hairline gold rules. The most grown-up and the most glamorous of the three. Botanicals show up as embroidery; vintage comes from the typography, not from paper.

III

The Conservatory

Plants as the spine — a Victorian glasshouse and the herbarium inside it. Deep green walls, brass glazing bars, and every show pinned to a cream specimen card like a pressed flower, with its own accession number. This one leans hardest into the greens and plants you said you love, and it's the most genuinely old-feeling of the three.
